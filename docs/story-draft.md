# Lumen — narrative draft for discussion

This draft captures the creator's stated motivation. It is a proposal for the README's opening, not a claim about machine consciousness.

## Possible opening

As more of our lives move onto screens, can a digital encounter still offer a little comfort?

Lumen begins with that question. It is an experiment in artificial life: a small, embodied presence you can spend time with. You can speak to it, approach it with your hand, or simply watch it wander and rest. Its form changes through repeated encounters, leaving a visible trace of the relationship you build.

Behind it is another question: what changes when AI has a body—something that can respond to proximity, touch, time, and your return?

Lumen does not establish that AI is conscious or that its responses are subjective feelings. It gives these questions a form we can encounter. The hope is that, in an increasingly digital world, a small artificial life might make room for curiosity, care, and a moment of comfort.

## Suggested README structure

1. A short opening: the human motivation and the question above.
2. Experience Lumen: live link, Talk to Lumen, Enable touch, and permission/privacy essentials.
3. What makes it an artificial-life experiment: an embodied response, its own rhythm, memory and gradual development.
4. What it does and does not claim: simulated behavior versus the open question of subjective experience; current prototype limits.
5. Run locally: dependencies, server-only credential setup, commands.
6. Technical details: a concise architecture overview, with extended implementation and testing notes in separate documents.

## Editorial direction

Lead with the experience and the creator's curiosity. Introduce models, thresholds, audio codecs and regression details only after readers understand why Lumen exists. Present comfort as an intention rather than a guaranteed outcome, and consciousness as an open question rather than a demonstrated capability.
